At 6:42 in the morning, Mom and Dad stood beside Noodles’s bed.

Noodles wore pajamas, a sleep mask, and the expression of someone who had no intention of participating in daylight.

Mom whispered, “We need the credit card.”

Dad whispered back, “I need a caramel latte. I cannot function past eight o’clock without one.”

“It isn’t eight yet.”

“That is how urgent this is.”

The credit card had disappeared the night before. Mom remembered placing it on the table. Noodles had been sitting nearby with a tablet and several travel websites open.

This did not prove anything.

It merely proved everything.

Mom gently touched the edge of the blanket.

Noodles pulled it higher.

“Do not disturb royalty,” she mumbled.

“We need the card,” Mom said.

Noodles spoke from beneath the sleep mask.

“I already used it.”

The room became silent.

Dad turned toward the bed.

“Used it for what?”

“Vacation clothes.”

Mom stared at the tablet. The shopping cart contained tropical dresses, sunglasses, sandals, an enormous sun hat, three swimsuits, and a beach bag embroidered with the word **ROYALTY**.

“I already have clothes for the trip,” Mom said.

Noodles lifted the sleep mask just enough to look at her.

“You packed the same capris you wore last time.”

Mom looked offended.

“They are comfortable.”

“They are recurring characters.”

Dad covered a laugh with a cough.

Mom asked where the card was now.

Noodles pointed one sleepy paw toward the nightstand.

It was beneath a confirmation page for express shipping.

Dad picked it up.

“Excellent. Now can we get the latte?”

“After we cancel the order,” Mom said.

Noodles lowered her sleep mask.

“You cannot cancel resort wear. It damages morale.”

Mom reviewed the order while Dad located his shoes and Noodles drifted back into dreams of beaches, room service, and arriving in a sun hat large enough to require its own luggage.

Operation: Retrieve the Credit Card had succeeded.

Operation: Stop the Tropical Wardrobe had already failed.
