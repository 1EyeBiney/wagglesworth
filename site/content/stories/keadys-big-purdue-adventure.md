Keady arrived at Purdue expecting a temporary camp job.

Within two days, the university appeared to need him permanently.

The large brown bear stood near campus in his Purdue sweater and helped students find everything they had misplaced.

“Where is the library?” one student asked.

Keady pointed the way.

“Where did I park?” asked another.

Keady helped retrace the route.

“Where can I get snacks?” asked nearly everyone.

That question he could answer immediately.

Back at home, the household followed his adventures by phone. Buttons requested daily security reports. Petunia wanted to know whether his room had a night-light. The Baroness asked whether Purdue was treating its newest representative with proper respect.

“They are,” Keady assured her.

Each day, the coach’s daughter came to visit him. She adored Keady and brought him a treat—sometimes a cookie, sometimes crackers, and once a small bag of candy that Buttons attempted to inventory through the phone.

“Two cookies should be sent home for evidence,” Buttons said.

“No,” said the Baroness. “One should be sent home as royal tax.”

Keady kept the cookies.

At camp, he became the person everyone trusted. Nervous students sat beside him. Lost families asked for directions. Staff members asked whether he could help organize supplies. By the end of the week, Keady knew the important buildings, the best parking routes, and the location of every snack machine.

Then Purdue made him an offer.

They wanted him to stay.

There would be a permanent position, continued work helping students, and a place to live with the coach’s family—the same family whose daughter already loved him and brought daily treats.

Keady called home.

The living room became silent when he told them.

Petunia’s tiny face fell. Buttons stopped making security notes. Mom and Dad were proud, but they knew how much the household would miss him.

The Baroness remained quiet longest.

“This is a significant opportunity,” she finally said.

“It is,” Keady replied.

“They need someone who can locate books, parking, and snacks.”

“Apparently.”

“And the child brings treats daily?”

“Yes.”

The Baroness sighed.

“Then you must go. It would be irresponsible to abandon such a well-organized snack arrangement.”

Keady laughed.

They made plans for calls and visits. Buttons established a Purdue branch of Royal Security. Petunia promised to mail drawings. The Baroness ordered Keady to remember where home was.

“The couch still has your section,” she told him.

“I thought we had not bought the sectional yet.”

“The concept of your section remains.”

Keady accepted the Purdue position.

He would help students, guide families, support the team, and live with people who already cared about him.

The household missed him, but they cheered him on.

Keady had arrived at Purdue for a temporary adventure.

He stayed because he had found another place where he was needed.
