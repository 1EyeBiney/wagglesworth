The Ladies-in-Waiting were going home for three days.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, approved the leave with a wave of one paw.

“The Estate will benefit from the quiet,” she said. “I may finally finish an entire cup of tea without an emergency involving glitter, roller skates, or a suspicious beverage.”

The departures began immediately.

Lady Piper and Lady Siren hugged everyone twice, left through the front doors, and returned five minutes later because they had forgotten their bags. Lady Moira carried home her mystery notes. Lady Lexie packed three books and a magnifying glass. Lady Special Sparkle required help closing a trunk full of gowns. Lady Virginia’s luggage clinked.

“Glassware,” Virginia explained.

Dame Muriel checked every name from the departure list. Lady Isla sang a farewell song in the drive. Lady Noodles Hobson tucked Petunia into a basket for one last hug before leaving. Lady Astrid skated toward her carriage until Countess Tilly reminded her that wheels and carriage steps were a poor combination.

At last, the drive was empty.

The Estate fell silent.

The Baroness drank half a cup of tea.

“Peaceful,” she said.

She drank the other half.

“Very peaceful.”

An hour later, she moved to the window.

“Were they meant to be gone this long?”

“They left one hour ago,” said Tilly.

“I was speaking generally.”

By dinner, nobody had scattered glitter in the dining room. Nobody had asked for a pawgarita. Nobody had rolled down the corridor. Nobody had whispered during announcements.

Buttons sat beside Esme and tried to entertain the room by performing a security demonstration with three dinner rolls.

It was not the same.

The next morning, the Baroness found herself counting the empty chairs at breakfast.

“Ten chairs are unnecessary,” she told Tilly. “Have them removed.”

The chairs were removed.

Twenty minutes later, the Baroness ordered them returned because the room looked “architecturally unbalanced.”

On the second day, she walked through the garden. The badminton court was empty. The croquet mallets stood untouched. The white roses bloomed beside silent paths.

Lady Isla was not there to sing. Piper and Siren were not whispering. Special Sparkle had left no glitter behind—except, somehow, everywhere.

“You miss them,” Tilly said.

“I miss activity.”

“Their activity.”

“Do not be dramatic.”

On the third morning, the Baroness took her tea to the front window.

Tilly joined her. Petunia sat on the sill. Esme waited beside Buttons, who claimed he was monitoring the driveway for security reasons.

Then came the sound of carriage wheels.

The Baroness stood so quickly that tea splashed into the saucer.

One carriage appeared. Then another.

The front doors opened, and the Estate filled with voices.

Piper and Siren rushed in talking at once. Moira brought new theories. Lexie had found another book about silver roses. Special Sparkle wore a gown that shed glitter with every step. Virginia carried fresh “glassware.” Dame Muriel had a complete home-leave report. Isla began singing. Lady Noodles Hobson hugged Petunia. Astrid rolled into the hall and nearly collided with Buttons.

The Baroness waited at the foot of the staircase, attempting to look unimpressed.

“You are all extremely loud,” she said.

“Did you miss us?” Piper asked.

“Certainly not.”

“Not even a little?” Siren asked.

The Baroness looked at the ten filled chairs, the glitter on the floor, the roller skates in the hall, and Lady Virginia already pouring something pink.

“The Estate,” she said carefully, “was becoming unbearably well-behaved.”

That was the closest the Baroness intended to come to admitting anything.

It was close enough.

The Ladies surrounded her in a laughing royal hug.

Buttons blew his whistle to celebrate their safe return.

Tilly took it away.

And Wagglesworth Estate was wonderfully noisy again.
