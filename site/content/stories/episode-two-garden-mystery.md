By ten o’clock the next morning, the Ladies-in-Waiting had searched every flower bed at Wagglesworth Estate.

This sounded more organized than it was.

Lady Piper and Lady Siren searched while whispering theories that became less sensible by the minute. Lady Moira checked beneath the roses. Lady Lexie inspected the hedges. Dame Muriel carried a notebook and tried to keep an official record, although Lady Special Sparkle kept leaving glittery pawprints on every page.

Lady Virginia brought a tray of “investigative refreshments.”

“Those are pawgaritas,” Tilly said.

“The investigation is outside,” Virginia replied. “Hydration matters.”

Lady Astrid wore roller skates until Countess Tilly pointed at the gravel path without saying a word. Astrid sighed and removed them.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, watched from beneath a lace parasol.

“We are looking for the place where flowers do not grow,” she reminded them. “Not reorganizing the entire kingdom one shrub at a time.”

Lady Isla wandered toward the oldest part of the garden, singing softly. Birds answered from the trees. Her song drifted over the roses, the lavender, and the tall purple foxgloves.

Then the birds stopped.

Isla stopped singing too.

Before her was a perfect circle of bare earth.

Flowers grew everywhere around it—white roses, pink peonies, bluebells, tiny golden blossoms—but nothing crossed the edge of that smooth, empty circle.

“Your Grace,” Isla called. “I believe the garden has been keeping a secret.”

Everyone crowded around.

Petunia arrived in a little basket carried by Esme, with her tiny legs hanging over the side. Buttons marched beside them and declared that he had secured the perimeter.

“You were following Esme,” said Tilly.

“Security requires flexibility.”

Esme smiled. Buttons forgot the next thing he was going to say.

In the center of the bare circle stood a flat gray stone. Lady Noodles Hobson brushed away the soil and discovered a silver rose carved into its surface.

“The same rose that was printed on the card,” she said.

Piper gasped. Siren gasped because Piper had gasped. Special Sparkle twirled so quickly that glitter flew from her gown and landed across the stone.

For one second, the carved rose glowed.

Everyone froze.

“Do it again,” whispered Astrid.

Special Sparkle sprinkled a little more glitter.

Nothing happened.

“Apparently the garden has standards,” said the Baroness.

Lady Moira knelt beside the stone and found a narrow line around its edge. With Lexie and Dame Muriel helping, they lifted it just enough to reveal a shallow compartment underneath.

Inside lay a tiny silver bell and another violet card.

The Baroness read aloud:

**WHEN TEN ARE GATHERED, RING ONLY ONCE. THE SECOND SECRET HIDES WHERE MUSIC HAS FORGOTTEN ITS SONG.**

Tilly counted the Ladies-in-Waiting twice.

“Ten,” she confirmed.

Buttons leaned forward. “As Head of Royal Security, I should ring bell.”

“As Chief of Paw-Operations,” said Tilly, “I should stop you.”

Petunia solved the argument by slipping from Esme’s basket, seizing the tiny bell with both hands, and ringing it once.

The sound was impossibly clear.

It traveled through the garden, over the fountains, and into the open windows of the Estate. A moment later, from somewhere inside the house, came the broken notes of a music box.

Three notes.

Then silence.

The ladies hurried inside, following the sound to a narrow morning room that had been closed for years. Dust covered the furniture. A faded portrait hung crookedly above the fireplace. On a side table sat an old silver music box decorated with tiny roses.

Lady Lexie wound the key.

The music box played the same three notes and stopped.

Lady Moira noticed that one silver rose on the lid would not move with the others. She pressed it, and a hidden drawer sprang open.

Inside was not a treasure.

It was a keyhole.

“A keyhole without a key,” Piper said.

“Or a key that has not found us yet,” said Siren.

Behind them, the crooked portrait moved.

Only an inch.

But it moved.

Buttons blew his security whistle so loudly that everyone jumped.

“Intruder!” he shouted.

“It is a painting,” Tilly said.

“Suspicious painting.”

The Baroness stepped closer and straightened the frame. Hidden behind it was a line of writing scratched into the wall:

**THE ROSE OPENS ONLY FOR THOSE WHO RETURN.**

Nobody understood what it meant.

Before they could decide, a palace footman entered with an urgent message. Every Lady-in-Waiting had been granted a short home leave before the next royal gathering.

The Baroness read the note, then looked at the hidden message.

“Those who return,” she repeated.

Tilly’s ears lifted.

The mystery was asking them to leave.

And then come back.

The Baroness smiled slowly.

“Ladies,” she announced, “pack lightly. We have a mystery waiting for your return—and if any of you bring twelve trunks for two days, you will carry them yourselves.”

From the old music box came one last silver note.

This time, it sounded almost like laughter.
