The weather report said seventy degrees and sunny.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth interpreted this as a royal invitation.

She put on her unicorn tutu, stepped into the yard, and began dancing.

Petunia stood near the door wearing a tiny scarf over her nose.

“The pollen is aggressive,” she warned. “Tree dandruff everywhere.”

Petunia sneezed so hard that her scarf turned sideways.

Keady sat on the patio eating crackers.

Buttons watched the Baroness spin beneath the trees.

“Weather ballet,” he announced.

The Baroness twirled again. Her tutu sparkled in the sunlight. Birds sang. Flowers opened. For one glorious moment, spring appeared to understand proper presentation.

Then the Baroness stepped into wet mulch.

Her paws slid forward.

Her tutu flew upward.

She landed in a flower bed with one leg extended and both ears pointing in different directions.

Everyone froze.

Buttons lowered his crackers.

Petunia stopped sneezing.

Keady waited.

The Baroness slowly rose.

“That,” she announced, “was intentional.”

Buttons nodded solemnly.

“Advanced weather ballet.”

Petunia sneezed again.

The Baroness attempted one more graceful turn. A gust of wind sent pollen across the yard. Petunia fled inside. Buttons’s hat blew into a bush. Keady continued eating crackers.

Mom called from the door, “How long are you all staying outside?”

Keady looked at the Baroness, who was preparing for another spin despite the mulch on her tutu.

“Not until she pulls a hamstring or the weather app changes.”

The Baroness glared at him.

A cloud crossed the sun.

The temperature seemed to drop three degrees.

The Baroness immediately walked toward the house.

“Spring has become unreliable,” she said.

Buttons retrieved his hat. Petunia waited inside with tissues. Keady carried the crackers.

The weather ballet ended after seven minutes, one sneeze attack, one mulch incident, and absolutely no admission of defeat.

The Baroness planned to try again in summer—once the ground had learned some manners.
