The family campfire crackled beside a quiet forest lake.

Everyone gathered near the flames—everyone except Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth.

Mom’s arthritis made it difficult to hold Noodles safely around the fire, so the Baroness had been placed several feet away in a chair of her own.

Buttons and Petunia had not come. Mom worried that they were too little for the dark ground, the open fire, and the constant feet moving around camp.

This left the Baroness alone.

She sat beneath the trees, watching the family roast marshmallows without her.

Beside her lay a completely unused, allegedly gold-plated marshmallow holder.

“A fine royal tool,” she muttered, “wasted by poor staffing.”

Someone stood up near the fire.

The Baroness stiffened.

Someone sat down.

Another person walked toward the house.

Then someone else returned.

The family included several blind relatives, and the Baroness was small, low to the ground, and deeply suspicious of every approaching footstep.

“Marvelous,” she said. “I have been banished from the fire and placed in the center of a pedestrian obstacle course.”

From her chair, she listened to doors opening and closing at the house. Footsteps crossed the path. Chairs scraped. Someone searched for graham crackers. Someone else looked for chocolate.

The Baroness kept one eye on the campfire and one eye on the traffic.

When Mom called to ask whether she was comfortable, Noodles looked at the empty marshmallow holder.

“Comfortable is a strong word.”

The lake reflected the orange firelight. Laughter traveled through the trees. Although the Baroness was salty about her exile, she also loved the warmth of her family gathered nearby.

She wished she could sit closer.

She wished Buttons and Petunia were there.

Most of all, she wished someone would use the gold-plated marshmallow holder before the evening ended.

At last, Mom brought her a small safe treat and sat beside her for a while.

The Baroness leaned close.

The campfire still felt too far away, but the evening no longer felt quite so lonely.

On the ride home, Noodles developed a plan.

The next campfire would be indoors.

They would use a laundry basket, orange tissue paper, and a flashlight. Buttons and Petunia could attend. Nobody would risk stepping on anyone. The Baroness could sit directly beside the “flames,” and the gold-plated marshmallow holder would finally receive the respect it deserved.

It would not be a real campfire.

But it would be safe, warm, and properly royal.
