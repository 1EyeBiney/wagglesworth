The silver doors slammed shut behind them.

Everyone froze.

Buttons blew his security whistle.

“Intrusion!” he shouted.

“Buttons,” said Countess Tilly, “we are the intruders.”

“Then security is working.”

Before them stretched the hidden ballroom of Wagglesworth Estate.

It was enormous—and completely forgotten.

Dust clouded the marble floor. The curtains hung gray and torn. Silver vines wrapped the walls but had lost their shine. A great chandelier sagged overhead, dark and silent. Along the edges of the room stood dozens of music boxes, each covered in years of dust.

At the far end, on a raised pedestal, rested a metal rose.

Its petals were dull silver.

Lady Rosalind appeared beside the doors, still wearing her faded blue gown and key-shaped crown.

“The Silver Rose,” she said. “When it awakens, the ballroom will remember what it once was.”

Lady Virginia looked around hopefully. “Does it remember where the bar was?”

“No bar,” said Tilly.

Virginia studied her small empty glass with deep sadness.

The Baroness held up the golden key. It pointed toward the center of the room like the needle of a compass.

The Ladies-in-Waiting moved forward together.

Lady Piper and Lady Siren held paws. Lady Moira and Lady Lexie studied every carving along the floor. Dame Muriel copied the symbols into her journal. Lady Isla sang the three notes from the garden music box. Lady Noodles Hobson carried a little basket containing Petunia, whose tiny legs hung over the edge. Lady Esme walked beside Buttons, both dressed in their finest royal clothes. Lady Special Sparkle’s silver gown lit the dusty floor wherever she stepped.

Lady Astrid rolled quietly behind them.

Tilly turned.

Astrid smiled.

“The staircase rule is over.”

Before Tilly could answer, Astrid’s skate crossed a thin silver line in the floor.

Every music box in the ballroom opened at once.

One played three notes.

Another answered.

Then another.

Soon a delicate melody traveled around the room, box to box, until it reached the pedestal.

The golden key flew from the Baroness’s paw and slipped into a hidden lock beneath the Silver Rose.

It turned.

Nothing happened.

Buttons leaned close. “Perhaps needs stronger bear.”

“Perhaps it needs the rest of the clue,” said Lady Moira.

Dame Muriel opened her journal and read every message they had found:

“Where the flowers do not grow, the first secret waits below. When ten are gathered, ring only once. The second secret hides where music has forgotten its song. The Rose opens only for those who return.”

“We did all of that,” Piper said.

“We returned,” Siren agreed.

Petunia climbed out of the basket.

Before anyone could stop her, the tiny doll scrambled up the pedestal and sat beside the Silver Rose like a very small queen.

The rose gave a faint glow.

Lady Noodles Hobson placed one paw on the pedestal. Lady Isla joined her. Then Moira, Lexie, Dame Muriel, Piper, Siren, Special Sparkle, Virginia, and Astrid gathered around it.

Ten Ladies-in-Waiting.

Esme touched the base. Buttons placed his paw beside hers. Tilly joined them.

The Baroness remained a few steps away.

Lady Rosalind looked at her.

“The Estate did not awaken for a title,” she said softly. “It awakened for its heart.”

The Baroness sighed with great royal inconvenience and stepped forward.

“If this ruins my manicure,” she said, placing her paw on the silver pedestal, “the magic will be receiving a formal complaint.”

The Silver Rose glowed.

At first, the light was gentle.

Then every candle in the ballroom lit at once.

The chandelier overhead flickered—and burst into golden light.

The old curtains straightened and shimmered into luxurious soft-pink velvet, glowing warmly in the candlelight. Silver vines brightened along the walls. Roses appeared everywhere: pale pink, white, and deep royal purple. Dust vanished from the marble floor. The music boxes played together, filling the ballroom with the song that had been missing for generations.

The Ladies-in-Waiting gasped.

Special Sparkle twirled until glitter flew from her gown, and the glitter became silver stars above them.

Virginia’s empty glass filled itself with a pink pawgarita.

“I knew this room had potential,” she whispered.

Astrid rolled in a joyful circle. Buttons raised his whistle, saw Tilly looking at him, and quietly hid it behind his back.

The door at the opposite side of the ballroom suddenly opened.

Everyone froze again.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, stood there.

Or rather, everyone *thought* she stood there—until they looked beside the pedestal and realized the Baroness was still with them.

The figure in the doorway was a silver reflection of Her Grace, dressed as the first Baroness of Wagglesworth had been long ago. She looked at the glowing ballroom. She looked at the Silver Rose. She looked at the ladies. She looked at Astrid’s roller skates. She looked at Buttons holding a whistle he had apparently forgotten to hide. She looked at Virginia’s glass.

Then she looked at Petunia, sitting beside the Silver Rose like a tiny queen.

The silver figure smiled.

“The Rose is restored,” she said. “Wagglesworth remembers.”

She vanished into a shower of silver light.

Lady Rosalind’s faded blue gown turned bright and new. Her key crown shone. For the first time, she laughed.

The Baroness looked around the transformed ballroom.

“There is only one sensible response to all of this,” she announced.

Tilly braced herself.

“We are having a ball.”

Piper squealed. Siren hugged her. Special Sparkle began planning gowns immediately. Dame Muriel opened a fresh page in her journal. Buttons volunteered to secure the refreshments. Tilly rejected that offer before he finished making it.

“The Enchanted Silver Rose Ball,” declared the Baroness. “One last magical night together. The first grand celebration in this ballroom since the Rose was lost—and all of Wagglesworth Estate shall be invited.”

The Silver Rose glowed gently beneath the chandelier.

Petunia settled more comfortably on the pedestal.

The Baroness looked at the tiny doll.

“And there it is.”
