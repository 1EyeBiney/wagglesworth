The Grand Drawing Room at Wagglesworth Estate had been prepared for a perfectly respectable afternoon tea.

That was the first warning that something unusual was about to happen.

White roses filled the silver vases. Tiny cakes stood in neat rows. The best china waited on a long table beneath the windows, and every chair had been arranged with royal precision by Countess Tilly Toodlefluff, Chief of Paw-Operations.

“If anyone moves one of those chairs,” Tilly announced, “I shall know.”

Buttons the Bear, Head of Royal Security, slowly removed his paw from the nearest chair.

Tiny Petunia sat inside a teacup, because she was a very small doll and because nobody had yet found a royal rule specifically forbidding it.

Lady Esmeralda—Esme to her friends—stood near Buttons in a pearl-trimmed gown. Buttons tried not to stare. He failed so completely that even Petunia noticed.

Then the silver doors opened.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, entered in a deep-purple gown with a long train, her chin raised and her expression suggesting that the room had been fortunate to exist long enough to receive her.

“Good afternoon,” she said. “I see the cakes survived Buttons’s security inspection.”

Buttons straightened. “Da. Every cake has been thoroughly guarded.”

“With your mouth?” asked the Baroness.

Buttons looked offended. Tilly looked unconvinced.

One by one, the Ladies-in-Waiting arrived: Lady Piper Lynn, Duchess of Giggles; Lady Siren LeeAnn, Duchess of Smiles; Lady Moira Ann; Lady Lexie Marie; Lady Special Sparkle; Lady Virginia; Dame Muriel Katharina Noodles of Warwickshire; Lady Isla of the Highland Heather; Lady Noodles Hobson of Snuggleton Manor; and Lady Astrid, who arrived slightly late and suspiciously out of breath.

“Were you running in the corridor?” Tilly asked.

“No,” Astrid replied.

A single roller skate rolled through the doorway behind her.

Tilly closed her eyes.

The ladies settled around the table. Lady Isla admired the roses. Lady Special Sparkle turned in her chair and scattered a fine dusting of glitter across Dame Muriel’s sleeve. Lady Piper and Lady Siren were already whispering and giggling. Lady Virginia lifted a delicate glass filled with something pink and frosty.

The Baroness stared at it.

“Virginia,” she said, “that does not look like tea.”

“It is a pawgarita, Your Grace.”

“It is three o’clock in the afternoon.”

“Exactly. I feared I was late.”

The Baroness considered this, then signaled for one to be brought to her.

When everyone had a cup, glass, plate, or—in Petunia’s case—an entire seat inside the china, the Baroness tapped one spoon against her teacup.

“Ladies, members of the household, suspicious bears, and tiny persons currently sitting in my best porcelain,” she began, “I have gathered you here because Wagglesworth Estate has been far too quiet.”

Tilly glanced toward the roller skate.

“Quiet is not the word I would have chosen,” she murmured.

“This summer,” the Baroness continued, “you will remain at the Estate as my Ladies-in-Waiting. There will be royal duties, garden parties, games, formal dinners, mysteries, and absolutely no unauthorized touching of ancient objects.”

Buttons immediately looked toward a locked display cabinet.

“I said no.”

Buttons looked away.

Piper clapped her paws. Siren squealed. Lady Special Sparkle tossed glitter into the air. Tilly lunged forward with a napkin before it landed in the frosting.

“There will also,” said the Baroness, “be standards.”

The room became quieter.

“Breakfast will be served at a civilized hour. Formal gowns will not be worn in the pond. Roller skates are not permitted on the grand staircase. And if Lady Virginia turns the lemonade fountain into a pawgarita fountain again, she must at least tell me first.”

Lady Virginia nodded solemnly.

As the laughter faded, Lady Isla noticed something tucked beneath the largest vase of white roses.

“Your Grace,” she said, “was this meant to be part of the table?”

She drew out a small card made of heavy silver paper.

On it, in faded violet ink, were the words:

**WHERE THE FLOWERS DO NOT GROW, THE FIRST SECRET WAITS BELOW.**

Nobody spoke.

Then Petunia climbed out of the teacup and marched across the table to inspect it.

Buttons reached for the card.

Tilly slapped his paw away.

“Ancient object,” she reminded him.

The Baroness took the card and read it again. For one brief moment, her usual snarky expression softened into curiosity.

“Well,” she said, setting down her pawgarita, “it appears Wagglesworth Estate has decided to entertain us.”

Outside the tall windows, the white roses in the garden moved in a breeze that nobody else could feel.

And somewhere deep inside the Estate, something made a faint silver chime.

The royal summer had begun.
