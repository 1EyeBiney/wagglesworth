Wagglesworth Estate had a seating crisis.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, Buttons, and Tiny Petunia had once fit comfortably on the little white bench.

Then Keady arrived.

Keady was a large brown bear. He was kind, calm, and shaped approximately like an entire additional piece of furniture.

The Baroness sat squeezed against one arm of the bench. Buttons was wedged beside her. Petunia occupied the only remaining three inches. Keady stood nearby because sitting down would have created a household emergency.

Mom looked at the group.

“You need a larger couch.”

The Baroness corrected her immediately.

“We need a proper wrap-around sectional with corners, personal space, snack access, and circulation.”

Buttons nodded. “Also security lane.”

“No,” said the Baroness. “You would use it to reach the snacks first.”

Mom pointed out that Keady was the reason they had outgrown the bench.

Keady looked apologetic.

“I can stand.”

“Nonsense,” said the Baroness. “You are not the problem. You are the expansion.”

Petunia raised both arms in agreement.

The Baroness began describing her required sectional: one corner for royal reclining, one for Buttons and his suspicious plotting, one safe Petunia-sized space, and one large Purdue section for Keady.

Mom asked where Dad was supposed to sit.

The Baroness paused.

“We may need an ottoman.”

Dad, listening from his chair, declined the offer.

Buttons tried to squeeze Keady onto the tiny bench for a test.

The bench creaked.

Petunia disappeared between two bears.

The Baroness’s bow tilted sideways.

“Stop immediately,” she ordered. “My household now resembles luggage.”

Keady stood again and lifted Petunia safely onto his shoulder.

Mom agreed to begin looking for a bigger couch.

The Baroness smoothed her bow and reclaimed three inches of space.

“For the record,” she announced, “we are not crowded.”

Buttons looked at Keady.

Keady looked at the tiny bench.

Petunia looked down from Keady’s shoulder.

The Baroness lifted her chin.

“We are expanding.”
