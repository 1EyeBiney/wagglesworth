For two whole days, Wagglesworth Estate was quiet.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, hated it.

She would never have admitted this, of course. She told everyone that the absence of the Ladies-in-Waiting had brought “a brief and welcome return to civilization.”

Then she checked the front drive eleven times to see whether they were coming back.

Countess Tilly noticed.

“You miss them,” Tilly said.

“I miss having witnesses when Buttons does something foolish.”

Across the terrace, Buttons was wearing a bathing cap and attempting to inflate a pool float with a bellows.

“Fair,” said Tilly.

Since the Estate was temporarily free of royal duties, the Baroness and Tilly declared the afternoon a private pool retreat. Two cabana attendants brought chilled drinks, pink towels, and a silver tray of fruit.

“This,” said the Baroness, settling beneath a purple umbrella, “is how responsible leaders recover from leadership.”

“You have not done anything since breakfast,” said Tilly.

“Exactly. I am recovering beautifully.”

Petunia floated past them in a teacup tied to a toy sail. Esme sat at the pool’s edge in a tiny sun hat. Buttons, trying to look impressive in front of her, stepped onto a large swan float.

The swan flipped.

Buttons disappeared into the water.

Esme laughed so sweetly that Buttons climbed back onto the float and fell in a second time on purpose.

“Royal Security has been compromised,” said the Baroness.

“By love,” Tilly whispered.

Buttons surfaced. “I heard that.”

The next morning, carriages began rolling through the gates.

The Ladies-in-Waiting had returned.

Piper and Siren ran up the steps at the same time and became stuck in the doorway. Lady Moira brought carefully packed notes about the mystery. Lady Lexie brought a magnifying glass. Special Sparkle brought three new glitter gowns and no practical shoes. Lady Virginia arrived with a traveling case that clinked suspiciously. Dame Muriel brought a proper leather-bound journal. Lady Isla brought pressed heather. Lady Noodles Hobson brought a soft blanket “in case the mystery required snuggling.” Astrid brought roller skates.

“Those were specifically not on the packing list,” Tilly said.

“They packed themselves,” Astrid replied.

The Baroness stood at the top of the steps, pretending not to be pleased.

“You are late,” she announced.

“We are early,” said Dame Muriel.

“Then you are late for being earlier.”

Nobody understood that, but everyone was too happy to argue.

Before returning to the music-box mystery, the Baroness ordered a welcome-back croquet match on the south lawn. Teams were chosen with great ceremony and no fairness at all.

Lady Moira and Lady Lexie played with quiet precision. Piper and Siren giggled through every turn. Special Sparkle’s ball became coated in glitter and was visible from nearly anywhere on the Estate. Lady Isla sang encouragement to hers. Lady Noodles Hobson somehow finished half the course while carrying Petunia in a little sling. Dame Muriel insisted on following the official rules. Astrid attempted to strike her ball while rolling past on skates.

Lady Virginia stood beside the final wicket holding a glass.

“Is that another investigative refreshment?” asked Tilly.

“This is competitive hydration.”

Buttons served as referee. Unfortunately, he spent most of the game watching Esme.

“Buttons,” called the Baroness, “did Astrid’s ball pass through the wicket?”

Buttons continued gazing at Esme.

“Buttons?”

Esme waved one tiny paw.

Buttons startled and blew his whistle. “Point for everyone!”

Tilly stared at him.

“There are rumors,” Piper whispered to Siren.

“Very romantic rumors,” Siren whispered back.

Buttons pretended not to hear. His ears turned pink beneath his security cap.

At last, the Baroness struck the winning ball through the final wicket.

“Naturally,” she said. “Royal skill.”

“You moved the wicket,” Tilly said.

“Royal strategy.”

As everyone laughed, the old music box inside the Estate began to play.

This time it did not stop after three notes.

The melody traveled through the open doors and across the lawn. The Ladies-in-Waiting looked at one another.

They had left.

They had returned.

And the Estate had noticed.

Everyone hurried to the morning room. The music box was open. Where the empty keyhole had been, a tiny golden key now rested on red velvet.

A tag had been tied around it.

In neat violet letters it said:

**DO NOT TOUCH THE MYSTERIOUS KEY.**

Piper leaned closer.

Siren leaned closer too.

Lady Virginia raised her glass.

Buttons reached out one paw.

“Don’t,” said every lady at once.

The key gave a tiny golden flash.

And somewhere behind the walls, an old lock turned.
