Mom announced that she was making Buckeyes for Christmas.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, immediately appointed herself Executive Director of Chocolate Operations.

Buttons the Bear became Head of Peanut Butter Security.

Tiny Petunia became Quality Control, mostly because she was small enough to stand beside the candy tray without blocking anyone’s view.

Mom mixed peanut butter, powdered sugar, and butter into a smooth filling.

The Baroness leaned over the bowl.

“It needs elegance.”

“It needs to be rolled into balls,” Mom said.

“That is what I meant.”

Buttons volunteered to roll the first one. His Buckeye came out shaped like a potato.

The Baroness stared at it.

“Is that candy or a threat?”

“Rustic,” Buttons replied.

Petunia rolled one so tiny that Mom almost mistook it for a crumb.

“Petunia-sized,” said the Baroness approvingly.

Soon the tray held rows of peanut-butter balls—some round, some lumpy, and one that Buttons insisted was shaped like the state of Ohio.

Mom placed the tray in the refrigerator to chill.

Buttons stood guard outside the door.

“Nobody enters,” he announced.

The Baroness walked past him and opened the refrigerator.

“Royal clearance.”

“That is not how security works.”

“It is exactly how royalty works.”

When the candy was firm, Mom melted the chocolate. Each peanut-butter ball was dipped almost to the top, leaving a small circle uncovered so it looked like a real Buckeye.

The Baroness watched the glossy chocolate settle.

“Now they are properly dressed.”

Buttons dipped his potato-shaped candy. It fell completely into the bowl.

He reached for it.

Mom stopped him.

“No bear paws in the chocolate.”

Petunia produced a tiny spoon and helped rescue it.

By the end, chocolate dotted the counter, powdered sugar dusted Buttons’s sweater, and one perfect fingerprint appeared in the corner of the Baroness’s apron.

Mom arranged the finished Buckeyes on a Christmas plate.

The Baroness counted them.

Then counted again.

“One is missing.”

Buttons looked innocent.

Petunia pointed at the smear of chocolate beneath his nose.

“Security sample,” Buttons said.

Mom laughed and handed one Buckeye to each helper.

The Baroness took one bite and closed her eyes.

“Excellent. Rich, elegant, and just slightly excessive.”

“Like you?” Mom asked.

The Baroness lifted her chin.

“Exactly like me.”

The Royal Buckeye Factory closed for the evening with a full candy plate, a messy kitchen, and one bear permanently removed from Quality Control.
