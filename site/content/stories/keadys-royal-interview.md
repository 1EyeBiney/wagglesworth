The interview was held at the tiny white bench in the living room.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, sat behind it as though it were a palace press-conference table.

Buttons the Bear sat on her right with an imaginary clipboard.

Tiny Petunia sat on her left with an even smaller imaginary clipboard.

Before them stood the newest arrival: a large brown bear wearing a Purdue sweater.

“State your name,” the Baroness said.

“Keady.”

Buttons wrote something imaginary.

“Key-dee?” he asked.

“Pronounced Katie,” Keady explained.

The Baroness stared.

Petunia tilted her head.

Buttons crossed out his imaginary notes.

“This will confuse everyone,” he said.

“He is named after Gene Keady,” Mom explained from across the room.

Dad nodded. Mom and Dad loved basketball, and a big Purdue bear named Keady had already earned their attention.

The Baroness was not ready to approve anyone so easily.

“What qualifications do you bring to Wagglesworth Estate?”

Keady thought carefully.

“I am large enough to reach high shelves. I am good at listening. I know a great deal about Purdue basketball. And I can sit very still during long meetings.”

The Baroness glanced at Buttons, who had tipped his chair backward twice in the last minute.

“That final skill is badly needed.”

Buttons leaned forward.

“Can new bear perform security?”

“Yes.”

“Can new bear share snacks?”

“Yes.”

“Can new bear take over couch?”

Keady looked at the tiny bench.

“I think the couch may already be in danger.”

Petunia nodded and made an imaginary checkmark.

The Baroness asked the most important final question.

“Who is your favorite Purdue player?”

Keady gave a diplomatic answer about the whole team.

The Baroness leaned back.

“Acceptable. I personally admire Fletcher Loyer, but I respect your caution.”

Mom asked whether that meant Keady could stay.

The Baroness looked at the large new bear, then at Buttons and Petunia.

Keady was much bigger than the others. He took up space. He brought new energy. He also looked extremely comfortable in a Purdue sweater.

“Wagglesworth Estate could use an expansion,” she decided.

Dad smiled.

Buttons stamped the imaginary paperwork.

Petunia climbed onto Keady’s paw to welcome him.

“You may stay,” the Baroness announced. “But understand that I remain Supreme Ruler of Fabulousness.”

Keady nodded.

“Understood, Your Grace.”

That answer sealed the decision.

The interview ended, the family gained one very large bear, and the tiny white bench immediately became much too small.
