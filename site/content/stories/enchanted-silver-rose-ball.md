Wagglesworth Estate had survived mysteries, magic, glitter, a possibly cursed staircase, and Buttons’s security whistle.

Whether it would survive preparations for the Enchanted Silver Rose Ball remained uncertain.

The hidden ballroom shone in silver, deep royal purple, and soft pink. Hundreds of candles waited along the walls. Roses climbed the silver vines. The chandelier sparkled above the polished floor.

Countess Tilly directed the preparations with three lists, two pencils, and the expression of someone holding civilization together by one thread.

Lady Special Sparkle supervised glitter.

Tilly had not assigned her that job.

Lady Virginia supervised the pawgarita fountain.

Tilly had not assigned her that job either.

Lady Astrid tested the dance floor on roller skates. Lady Isla rehearsed with the musicians. Dame Muriel checked the guest list. Lady Moira and Lady Lexie examined the final Silver Rose clues. Lady Noodles Hobson helped Esme dress Tiny Petunia in a miniature silver-and-pink ball gown.

Buttons wore a formal royal kilt and guarded the ballroom doors, although he kept finding reasons to walk past Esme.

Meanwhile, Lady Piper and Lady Siren were sent to collect ribbon from the west corridor.

As they passed the Baroness’s private sitting room, they heard laughter behind the partly open door.

They stopped.

Inside, Her Grace was speaking with her dear friend Lulabelly.

“Thank you so much for keeping the secret of my name,” the Baroness said. “It is so funny that everyone is calling me ‘Your Grace’ when my name is actually Grace.”

The Baroness giggled.

Piper’s eyes widened.

Siren’s mouth fell open.

They tiptoed away with the ribbon—and the most important secret in the entire Estate.

They went straight to high tea.

“We must not tell anyone,” Piper whispered.

“Absolutely no one,” Siren agreed.

Then they told Lady Virginia because she was sitting beside them.

Virginia told Special Sparkle, who gasped so loudly that Dame Muriel heard. Dame Muriel mentioned it to Moira in the interest of accurate recordkeeping. Moira asked Lexie whether she thought it was true. Lexie told Lady Isla only because Isla promised to sing quietly about it. Lady Noodles Hobson overheard the song. Astrid rolled past at exactly the wrong moment. Buttons learned it during a security check. Esme heard Buttons practicing how to say, “Lady Grace,” without fainting.

By sunset, everyone in Wagglesworth Castle knew the Baroness’s secret name.

Everyone except the Baroness.

That evening, the guests arrived.

The Ladies-in-Waiting entered in elegant gowns of silver, purple, and soft pink. Tilly wore a magnificent royal dress. Buttons looked very proud of his kilt. Esme sparkled in pearls. Petunia rode in a tiny flower-trimmed basket until she slipped away unnoticed.

Then the ballroom doors opened.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, appeared in an elaborate gown with enormous soft-pink skirts, silver embroidery, touches of royal purple, and a very, very long train. Her hair rose in an elegant royal style, with the Silver Rose nestled into it beneath a shining tiara.

The room bowed.

The Baroness looked pleased.

“You may all rise,” she said. “It is difficult to admire my gown while everyone is staring at the floor.”

Music filled the ballroom.

The dancing began.

Piper and Siren swept across the floor, trying not to giggle whenever anyone said “Your Grace.” Special Sparkle twirled until glitter flew from her gown. Lady Isla sang with the orchestra. Astrid danced on skates so beautifully that even Tilly stopped objecting. Lady Virginia discovered that the magical pawgarita fountain refilled itself.

Buttons offered one paw to Esme.

“For security,” he explained.

“Of course,” Esme said, and danced with him anyway.

Near midnight, the music boxes around the ballroom suddenly stopped.

The Silver Rose dimmed.

Lady Rosalind appeared beside the pedestal.

“One secret remains,” she said. “Until it is freely given, the Rose cannot complete its light.”

Moira and Lexie hurried forward with the collected clue cards. Dame Muriel opened her journal.

“A secret freely given,” Moira said. “Not stolen. Not guessed.”

“Something hidden in plain sight,” Lexie added.

From the crowd came a tiny metallic click.

Petunia had quietly slipped a little silver key into her pocket earlier that day. Now she climbed the pedestal and held it up.

The key was engraved with one word:

**GRACE.**

Silence spread across the ballroom.

Lady Esme stepped forward.

“Your Grace,” she said gently, “I have to ask. It is supposed to be a secret, but word around the castle is that your name is actually Grace. Is it true?”

The Baroness slowly looked at Piper.

Piper looked at Siren.

Siren looked at Virginia.

Virginia took a drink.

The Baroness tried to look stern.

She lasted three seconds.

Then she laughed.

“Yes,” she said. “My name is Grace. I have spent the entire summer being called ‘Your Grace’ while waiting to see if any of you would notice.”

“We noticed,” Piper said.

“Eventually,” Siren added.

“And then informed the entire kingdom,” said Tilly.

The Baroness shook her head, smiling.

“Then let it no longer be a secret. I am Grace—Baroness Wagglesworth, Supreme Ruler of Fabulousness, snark queen of the living room, and very fortunate friend of every ridiculous soul in this ballroom.”

A single tear appeared in the Baroness’s eye.

Petunia placed the tiny silver key into the base of the Rose.

The Silver Rose burst into brilliant silver light.

The chandelier illuminated.

Hundreds of candles flickered at once.

The soft-pink curtains billowed.

Silver roses of light floated through the ballroom and settled above every guest like stars. The music boxes began playing the full lost melody, and Lady Rosalind’s silver form glowed brighter than ever.

“The mystery is complete,” she said. “The Rose did not need a royal title. It needed truth, friendship, and a heart willing to be known.”

The Baroness wiped away her tear before anyone could make a fuss.

“If this story includes me crying,” she warned, “I shall deny everything.”

“I have already written it down,” Dame Muriel said.

The dancing began again.

They danced beneath the silver light until the candles burned low. They laughed, toasted, sang, skated, whispered, and made enough noise to fill every forgotten room at Wagglesworth Estate.

At the end of the night, the ten Ladies-in-Waiting gathered around the Baroness. The royal summer was ending, but nobody felt that they were saying goodbye.

“You came here as Ladies-in-Waiting,” Grace told them. “You leave as part of Wagglesworth forever.”

Buttons blew his whistle in celebration.

Tilly took it away.

Petunia climbed back onto the pedestal beside the Silver Rose.

Lady Virginia raised one final pawgarita.

And Her Grace—whose name was finally and officially Grace—looked around at her Estate family and smiled.

The Silver Rose continued to glow.

It would remember them all.
