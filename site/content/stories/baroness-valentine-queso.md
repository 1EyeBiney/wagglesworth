Valentine’s Day arrived at Wagglesworth Estate with flowers, pink hearts, and entirely unreasonable expectations.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, sat in the living room wearing a red bow and waiting for tribute.

Buttons the Bear offered her a handmade card.

Petunia presented one tiny paper heart.

Dad wished her a happy Valentine’s Day.

The Baroness looked around.

“This is all very touching,” she said, “but where is dinner?”

Mom asked what Her Grace had in mind.

The Baroness had prepared a perfectly modest list: flowers, a comfortable seat, immediate service, and Mexican food.

Not just any Mexican food.

She received:

Chips.

Queso.

A steak quesadilla.

The Baroness stared at the feast with shining eyes.

“At last,” she whispered. “A Valentine who understands me.”

Buttons reached toward the chips.

The Baroness covered the basket with one paw.

“Those are part of my gift.”

“Bear only performing security check.”

“Your security checks always end with crumbs on your sweater.”

Petunia climbed onto the table and peered into the bowl of melted cheese.

Mom moved her back to safety.

“Petunia is too little to swim in the queso.”

“Nobody is swimming in it,” said the Baroness. “I am protecting the integrity of this queso.”

Dad sat down with his own dinner.

“I thought Valentine’s Day was about love,” he said.

“It is,” the Baroness replied. “Queso is a lifestyle, but it is also love.”

She lifted one chip, inspected the cheese coverage, and added a little more.

“Extra cheese,” she explained. “The heart wants what the heart wants.”

Buttons tried a different approach.

“If Baroness loves bear, Baroness shares chip.”

“If bear loves Baroness, bear orders his own queso.”

Petunia held up her tiny paper heart again.

The Baroness’s expression softened. She broke off the smallest safe corner of a plain chip and placed it beside Petunia.

“Very well. One royal Valentine portion.”

Buttons looked hopeful.

“You may have one too,” she said. “But keep your sweater out of the bowl.”

They ate together while Mom laughed and Dad listened to Buttons crunching much louder than necessary.

When the last piece of steak quesadilla disappeared, the Baroness leaned back with a satisfied sigh.

“Flowers wilt,” she announced. “Cards get put in drawers. But emotionally supportive cheese is there when you truly need it.”

Dad asked whether that meant queso was her real Valentine.

The Baroness looked at Mom, Dad, Buttons, and Tiny Petunia gathered around the table.

“My family is first,” she said. “But chips and queso are a close second.”
