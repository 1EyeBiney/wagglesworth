After the failure of Operation Pawgarita Patrol, Baroness Wagglesworth, Buttons, and Tiny Petunia made a new plan.

They would go outside dressed as ghosts and seek freedom.

The Baroness wore a white sheet with a purple bow. Buttons wore a sheet that was too short and exposed both furry feet. Petunia wore a handkerchief.

“Tonight,” the Baroness declared, “we go where the wind takes us.”

“To candy?” Buttons asked.

“Preferably.”

They marched toward the front door.

Mom saw them immediately.

“Where are you three going?”

The ghosts stopped.

“Nowhere,” said the Baroness.

Petunia was already holding a trick-or-treat bag.

“We are going to be free,” Buttons admitted.

Mom looked outside at the cold, dark evening. Then she looked at Petunia, who was small enough to vanish beneath one fallen leaf.

“You may be free inside.”

The front door remained closed.

The Baroness returned to the living room in offended silence.

“Our liberty has been restricted.”

Buttons arranged pillows like tombstones. Petunia draped black fabric over the coffee table. Together, they turned the living room into a tiny haunted graveyard.

Mom dimmed the lights. Dad supplied spooky sounds by tapping on the wall and making the Baroness jump twice.

“I was not frightened,” she insisted. “I was confirming the source.”

They placed a cheesecake in the middle of the room as the evening’s treasure.

Buttons guarded it with his full attention.

“Nothing passes.”

Petunia passed beneath his arm.

The Baroness passed behind him.

When Buttons turned around, one slice was already missing.

“Security breach!”

Dad listened from his chair.

“The breach sounds like it is chewing.”

The Baroness swallowed and looked innocent.

They spent the rest of the night telling ghost stories, guarding what remained of the cheesecake, and handing candy to one another from the same bowl.

At midnight, the Baroness lifted her fork.

“True freedom,” she announced, “is not about going outside.”

Buttons leaned closer.

“What is true freedom?”

“Dessert without supervision.”

Mom took away the cheesecake server.

The Baroness sighed.

Their search for freedom would continue another day.
