The invitation arrived on a Tuesday.

It was cream-colored, edged in gold, and sealed with purple wax. Across the front, in elegant writing, were the words:

**TO HER GRACE, BARONESS WAGGLESWORTH, AND THE ROYAL HOUSEHOLD.**

The Baroness placed it on the drawing-room table while she finished her tea.

“Nobody touch it,” she said.

Buttons the Bear stopped touching it.

“I was securing envelope.”

Countess Tilly put the invitation beneath a glass paperweight. Tiny Petunia climbed onto the table to inspect the seal. Lady Esme admired the gold border.

Dad, listening from his chair, asked the important question.

“What are you invited to?”

The Baroness lifted one paw.

“I shall open it after tea. Royal timing matters.”

Five minutes later, the invitation was gone.

The paperweight remained exactly where Tilly had placed it. The purple wax seal lay beside it. But the envelope and everything inside had vanished.

Buttons blew his whistle.

“Royal theft!”

“Indoor voice,” Dad said.

The Baroness stared at the empty table.

“Nobody leaves the room.”

Petunia was already halfway beneath the door.

“Especially Petunia.”

Tilly began the investigation. Buttons questioned everyone, although his questions were not particularly useful.

“Where were you at time of invitation?” he asked Esme.

“Beside you.”

Buttons blushed. “Excellent alibi.”

The Baroness examined the wax seal. A tiny white thread clung to one edge.

Tilly found a streak of gold dust on the carpet. Petunia discovered a small torn corner of cream paper beneath the couch.

The trail led out of the drawing room, through the hall, and into the kitchen.

There they found another scrap of the envelope beside the pantry door.

“A snack-related crime,” the Baroness said. “I should have known.”

Buttons opened the pantry.

Nothing appeared unusual—except for a faint rustling from the lowest shelf.

Dad tilted his head.

“The invitation thief is behind the cracker box,” he said.

Buttons looked impressed. “How does Dad know?”

“Because unlike Royal Security,” Dad replied, “I have been listening.”

Tilly moved the cracker box.

There sat Petunia.

“Petunia!” cried the Baroness.

The tiny doll held one piece of the invitation, but not the missing envelope. She pointed toward a narrow gap beneath the pantry cabinet.

From inside came another rustle.

Buttons reached beneath the cabinet and pulled out the true culprit: the household’s small cleaning brush, tangled in a long white dusting cloth. The cloth had caught on the invitation when a draft swept through the open window. It had dragged the envelope from beneath the paperweight, across the carpet, down the hall, and under the pantry cabinet.

“So there was no thief,” Esme said.

“There was wind,” Tilly replied.

“Suspicious wind,” Buttons said.

The envelope was wrinkled but readable. The Baroness opened it at last.

Inside was an invitation to a neighborhood card night, with snacks provided and guests encouraged to dress comfortably.

The Baroness read the final line twice.

“It says to bring a favorite game and a small dish to share.”

Dad smiled. “You could bring crackers.”

The Baroness looked toward the pantry.

The cracker box was empty.

Petunia hid one tiny crumb behind her back.

Buttons cleared his throat.

The Baroness narrowed her eyes.

The Case of the Vanishing Invitation had been solved.

The Case of the Vanishing Crackers was only beginning.
