The mysterious golden key spent the night beneath a glass dome in the Grand Drawing Room.

Countess Tilly placed a sign beside it:

**DO NOT TOUCH THE MYSTERIOUS KEY.**

Lady Piper added a second sign:

**SERIOUSLY. TILLY MEANS IT.**

Lady Siren added:

**NOT EVEN JUST A LITTLE TOUCH.**

Lady Virginia placed an empty glass beside the signs and wrote:

**NO TOUCHING UNTIL AFTER REFRESHMENTS.**

By breakfast, the table looked less like a royal exhibit and more like a very anxious yard sale.

Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, entered carrying her tea.

“Why,” she asked, “does the key now have more written instructions than Buttons?”

Buttons the Bear stood at attention beside Lady Esme. “I require no instructions.”

At that exact moment, he leaned against the display table. The glass dome wobbled.

Esme caught it with one tiny paw.

Buttons looked at her with complete admiration.

“Perhaps one instruction,” said Tilly.

The ten Ladies-in-Waiting gathered around the table. Lady Moira reviewed the clues in Dame Muriel’s journal. Lady Lexie examined the key through her magnifying glass. Lady Noodles Hobson held Petunia safely in the crook of one arm. Lady Isla hummed the music-box melody. Astrid wore one roller skate and claimed this technically obeyed the rule against roller *skates*.

Then Lady Special Sparkle entered.

Her gown was silver, enormous, and covered in so much glitter that daylight seemed to follow her into the room.

“Too much?” she asked, spinning once.

Glitter drifted onto the carpet, the table, Buttons’s cap, and Lady Virginia’s empty glass.

“Not if your goal is to be seen from another county,” said the Baroness.

The golden key flashed.

Lady Isla stopped humming.

Lady Special Sparkle spun again. The glitter on the key arranged itself into a tiny crown.

“A crown,” said Lady Moira. “The portrait upstairs—the tall lady in the faded blue gown. She is wearing a crown made of keys.”

The company hurried to the old portrait gallery.

The painting waited at the far end: a tall royal dog in a faded blue gown, one paw resting on a silver rose. Around her head was a delicate crown shaped from keys.

“Lady Rosalind,” Dame Muriel read from the brass nameplate. “Keeper of the Silver Rose.”

The Baroness lifted the mysterious key.

“If this portrait begins speaking,” she said, “Buttons is forbidden to blow that whistle.”

Buttons slowly lowered the whistle from his mouth.

Lady Lexie found a tiny keyhole hidden in the frame. The golden key fit perfectly.

It turned once.

The portrait swung open like a door.

Behind it stood Lady Rosalind herself.

Or perhaps it was her ghost.

Or perhaps Wagglesworth Estate had simply decided that paintings were optional.

She was tall and elegant, with long ears, a faded blue gown, and the same crown of golden keys. Silver light shimmered faintly around her.

Piper gasped.

Siren gasped.

Buttons forgot the no-whistle rule and blew one sharp note.

“I have waited a very long time,” Lady Rosalind said.

The Baroness raised one brow. “We had home leave.”

Lady Rosalind almost smiled.

She explained that long ago, Wagglesworth Estate had held a hidden ballroom. At its center stood the Silver Rose, an enchanted treasure that glowed when the Estate was filled with friendship, courage, and joy.

But the ballroom had been sealed. The music had stopped. The Rose had faded, and every key to the room had vanished—except the one that would return when ten loyal ladies left the Estate and chose to come back.

“That is why the clue said the Rose opens only for those who return,” said Lady Moira.

Lady Rosalind nodded.

“The key found you because you came back to one another.”

For once, the Baroness had no snarky answer.

She looked at her Ladies-in-Waiting, at Tilly, at Esme and Buttons, and at Petunia, who had climbed from Lady Noodles Hobson’s arm and was now peering through the open portrait door.

Petunia vanished through it.

“Petunia!” everyone cried.

They rushed after her.

Beyond the portrait was a narrow landing and a staircase curling downward into darkness. Dust covered the steps. Silver vines ran along the walls, ending in small metal roses.

Far below, two enormous silver doors reflected a faint blue light.

Lady Rosalind handed the golden key to the Baroness.

“The ballroom remembers,” she warned. “It will reveal what was lost—but only if the company enters together.”

“Is the staircase safe?” asked Dame Muriel.

Lady Rosalind paused.

“It is probably safe.”

“Probably?” Tilly repeated.

“There was an old rumor that it was possibly cursed.”

Astrid immediately put on her second roller skate.

“Absolutely not,” said Tilly.

Petunia stood halfway down the staircase, waving impatiently.

The Baroness gathered the long train of her purple gown and stepped through the portrait.

“Very well,” she said. “Ten Ladies-in-Waiting, one Countess, one tiny Yorkie, one suspicious bear, one extremely tiny doll, and one Baroness shall descend the possibly cursed staircase.”

Lady Virginia raised her empty glass.

“Will there be a bar?”

Lady Rosalind’s silver expression remained solemn.

“The ballroom has been sealed for generations.”

Virginia sighed. “So that is a no.”

Together, they began walking down.

When the last lady crossed the threshold, the portrait swung shut behind them.

The golden key grew warm in the Baroness’s paw.

Below, the great silver doors slowly opened.
