Noodles decided she needed a Halloween costume that was glamorous, frightening, and impossible to ignore.

Mom should have stopped her at “impossible to ignore.”

Using craft foam, makeup, glitter, and several decorations that had not been approved for facial use, the future Baroness created a homemade mask.

She applied dark color around the eyes, attached silver foam points to the sides, and added enough glitter to signal passing airplanes.

Then she walked silently into the kitchen.

Mom turned around.

Mom screamed.

Noodles screamed because Mom screamed.

Dad sat in the next room listening to NFB-NEWSLINE. He paused the daily news.

“What happened?”

Mom took one breath.

“Noodles is wearing a terrifying homemade mask.”

Dad, who is blind, tilted his head toward the kitchen.

“I can’t see the mask, but judging by the scream, I assume she achieved her artistic goal.”

Noodles stepped closer so Dad could feel the edge of the foam.

One glitter-covered point bent beneath his finger.

“Is this a mask,” he asked, “or did a craft store explode on her face?”

Noodles gave one offended bark.

Mom tried to remove the mask. Glitter transferred to her hands, her shirt, the counter, and somehow the refrigerator handle.

“This is not safe for trick-or-treating,” Mom said.

Noodles lowered her head.

She had wanted to wear it everywhere.

Mom studied the dramatic mask again.

“You may wear it to the family Halloween party,” she decided, “for pictures only—and we are fixing the loose foam first.”

Noodles perked up.

Dad resumed NFB-NEWSLINE.

“Please warn me before the craft monster enters the room again.”

At the party, Noodles posed proudly in her repaired mask. Everyone admired the glitter, the dramatic eyes, and the expression of a dog who believed she had invented Halloween.

Mom kept the glue nearby.

Dad stayed safely out of glitter range.

The Mask-tastrophe became a success—but only after the entire kitchen sparkled for three weeks.
