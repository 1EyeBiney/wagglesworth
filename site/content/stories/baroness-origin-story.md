Before there was a Wagglesworth Estate, before there were Ladies-in-Waiting, and before anyone had heard of a pawgarita fountain, Noodles was simply Noodles.

At least, that is what Dad believed.

Noodles knew better.

Every morning, she took her place in the grandest chair in the living room and waited for breakfast to be presented. If the bowl was late, she tapped one paw. If the serving was too small, she stared at Dad in wounded silence. If someone suggested she eat on the floor like an ordinary dog, she looked so offended that they immediately apologized.

Buttons the Bear sat on the couch nearby, studying her rise to power.

“She has no crown,” Buttons said. “No army. Only stare.”

Petunia, the tiny doll, nodded wisely. The stare was enough.

One afternoon, Dad sat down in Noodles’s chair.

Noodles stopped in the doorway.

The room became silent.

She looked at Dad.

She looked at the chair.

She looked back at Dad.

“Oh, I’m sorry,” Dad said. “Was this throne reserved for Her Royal Highness?”

Noodles did not move.

Dad sighed and shifted to the other end of the couch.

Noodles climbed into the chair, turned around exactly three times, and settled onto the cushion.

“There,” Dad said. “The Baroness of Wagglesworth has reclaimed her estate.”

Noodles’s ears lifted.

Baroness.

It sounded elegant. Important. Expensive.

Wagglesworth sounded like a place where the tea was properly served and nobody expected a lady to fetch her own snacks.

Buttons leaned toward Petunia.

“This is how governments fall,” he whispered.

The title might have remained a joke if Noodles had not immediately begun using it.

That evening, Dad was five minutes late with her treat.

Noodles sat tall in her chair and gave one commanding bark.

“Yes, Baroness Wagglesworth,” Dad said. “Your household staff has failed you.”

The next day, she refused to walk through a puddle.

“Of course not, Your Grace,” Dad told her. “The royal paws must remain dry.”

By the end of the week, Noodles had claimed the living room as her drawing room, the kitchen as her royal dining hall, the backyard as her private gardens, and Dad as the official Keeper of Snacks.

Countess Tilly became Chief of Paw-Operations because somebody had to keep the new government organized. Buttons appointed himself Head of Royal Security. Petunia received a title simply because she was tiny, fearless, and very difficult to find after she climbed into a handbag.

Noodles required one final thing: a proper royal name.

“Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth,” Dad announced, “Supreme Ruler of Fabulousness, snark queen of the living room, destroyer of squeaky toys, and connoisseur of snacks.”

Noodles approved.

From that day forward, she was no longer merely a dog sitting in someone else’s chair.

She was a Baroness sitting on her throne.

And the entire Estate has been living under her rule ever since.
