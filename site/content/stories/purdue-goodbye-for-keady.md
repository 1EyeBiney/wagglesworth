Keady’s Purdue sweater was folded neatly beside his travel bag.

For once, the household was quiet.

The large brown bear had been offered an opportunity at Purdue, and everyone knew he should go. Mom and Dad were proud. Buttons said it was an important assignment. Tiny Petunia had made him a goodbye card no larger than a playing card.

Even Her Grace, Baroness Wagglesworth, admitted that Purdue needed someone responsible.

“They have students, books, parking, and snacks,” she said. “Clearly they require professional supervision.”

But when the morning of Keady’s departure arrived, nobody felt ready.

Keady sat beside his bag. Petunia climbed onto his knee and held out her card. On the front she had drawn four figures sitting together on a couch much larger than their real one.

Inside were the words:

**SAVE YOUR SEAT.**

Keady hugged her carefully.

Buttons cleared his throat and handed Keady an official security badge made from cardboard.

“Temporary Purdue division,” Buttons explained. “Report suspicious activity. Especially missing snacks.”

Keady promised he would.

The Baroness stood a few steps away, pretending to inspect his packing.

“You have your sweater?”

“Yes.”

“Your phone?”

“Yes.”

“Emergency treats?”

“Buttons packed three bags.”

“He was supposed to pack two.”

Buttons looked away.

Mom reminded everyone that Keady was not disappearing. He would visit. He would call. He would still be part of the family.

Keady nodded.

“I promise visits, calls, and snacks.”

That made Petunia smile, but the Baroness’s eyes still looked softer than usual.

“Purdue is lucky to have you,” she said. “Do not let this go to your head. You remain subject to Wagglesworth authority during all visits.”

Keady smiled.

“Understood, Your Grace.”

They gathered around him for one long family hug—the Baroness pressed against one side, Buttons on the other, Petunia tucked safely between them, and Mom and Dad close by.

Then it was time.

Keady lifted his travel bag and put on his Purdue sweater.

He looked back at the tiny white bench where he had first been interviewed, the couch they had outgrown, and the family that had made room for him anyway.

“I’ll come home,” he promised.

The Baroness nodded because speaking had suddenly become inconvenient.

Buttons saluted.

Petunia waved her tiny card.

As Keady left for his new adventure, the whole household called after him:

“Boiler Up, Keady. We’ll save your seat until you come home.”
